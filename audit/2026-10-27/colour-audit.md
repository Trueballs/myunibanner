# University colour audit — 27 October 2026

49 schools reviewed, including the 22 currently unavailable schools. Henley is reviewed separately as a sub-brand.

## Evidence and limits

- 26 school palettes are supported by published institutional guidance. Some public manuals are dated; their dates and discrepancies are recorded below.
- 5 additional school palettes and Henley are corroborated against current official website CSS. This is narrower evidence than a brand manual.
- 18 schools remain unconfirmed. Current guidance was inaccessible or exact digital specifications were not established. These are not certified as correct.
- Known repeated blue/gold placeholders have been removed. Imperial and Reading use neutral black/white while their digital palettes remain unresolved. Other unconfirmed legacy palettes are retained, not newly verified.
- This audit covers background palettes, not approval of every logo/photo, asset licences, or full compliance with each university’s design rules.

## Three checks

1. Research sources: reject unrelated universities, old identities, print-only values and colours restricted to specialist use.
2. Data comparison: compare the original catalogue with source values; preserve the distinction between university and sub-brand. Remove stale per-logo background overrides.
3. Implementation verification: rebuild all 85 school/campus records; test palette inheritance, valid hex values, stale saved-draft handling and retained assets. Browser-check the page and representative editor palettes.

## Henley Business School

Website-confirmed palette: `#0A3B85`, `#FFCD00`, `#FFFFFF`. [Official homepage](https://www.henley.ac.uk) and its linked stylesheet identify brand-blue and brand-yellow. Kept separate from Reading.

## Maintenance

Edit `src/data/brand-colors.json`, then run `npm run build` and `npm test`. Do not edit generated catalogue colours directly. Add the source and a precise note when resolving a pending school. The build fails when a newly listed school has no colour review.

The JSON audit preserves before/after values. `source-verified` means supported by the cited guidance, not university endorsement.
